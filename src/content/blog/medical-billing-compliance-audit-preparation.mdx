Medicare and Medicaid audits are on the rise, with CMS reporting a 40% increase in billing audits over the past three years. For healthcare practices, an audit can mean substantial recoupments, penalties, and even legal action. This guide covers everything you need to know about billing compliance and audit preparation.

## Types of Medicare Audits

### Complex Medical Review (CMR)
Pre-payment review of claims based on identified risk areas. CMR adds significant delays to payment and requires extensive documentation submission.

### Targeted Probe and Educate (TPE)
Identifies providers with unusual billing patterns and provides education after review. Can escalate to more serious reviews if issues persist.

### Recovery Audit Contractor (RAC) Audits
Post-payment audits focused on identifying improper payments. RACs are paid on a contingency basis, creating aggressive review patterns.

### Comprehensive Error Rate Testing (CERT)
Random sample audits to calculate the Medicare fee-for-service improper payment rate. While smaller in scope, CERT findings can trigger targeted audits.

### Zone Program Integrity Contractor (ZPIC) Audits
Fraud-focused audits with potentially serious consequences including referrals to law enforcement.

## What Auditors Look For

### Hospice-Specific Audit Targets

- Documentation of terminal prognosis (6 months or less)
- Physician certification and recertification timing
- GIP level of care medical necessity
- Attending physician relationships
- Nursing facility billing coordination
- Hospice cap compliance

### Home Health-Specific Audit Targets

- Homebound status documentation
- Physician orders and face-to-face encounters
- OASIS accuracy
- Skilled care documentation
- Therapy utilization
- PDGM coding accuracy

### General Audit Risk Areas

- Medical necessity for all services
- Accurate ICD-10 and CPT/HCPCS coding
- Documentation supporting billed services
- Signed and dated orders
- Proper modifier usage

## Preparation Strategies

### 1. Implement Continuous Internal Auditing

Don't wait for external audits. Conduct internal audits monthly:
- Review 10-20 random claims per month
- Focus on high-risk billing areas
- Document findings and corrective actions
- Track trends over time

### 2. Maintain Pristine Documentation

Auditors' #1 finding is inadequate documentation. Every billed service must be supported by:
- Clear medical necessity justification
- Specific clinical observations
- Signed and dated physician orders
- Detailed visit notes (not template-only)
- Evidence of proper level of care

### 3. Establish a Compliance Program

CMS expects formal compliance programs including:
- Written policies and procedures
- Compliance officer designation
- Regular staff training
- Internal reporting mechanisms (hotline, etc.)
- Disciplinary policies for violations
- Regular internal monitoring
- Prompt investigation of potential issues

### 4. Train Your Staff Regularly

Billing regulations change constantly. Ensure staff receives:
- Annual compliance training
- Updates on regulatory changes
- Specialty-specific coding education
- Documentation training
- Fraud prevention awareness

### 5. Maintain an Audit-Ready File System

Organize records so that if audited, you can quickly provide:
- Complete patient records
- Financial and billing documentation
- Staff credentials and licenses
- Compliance program documentation
- Prior audit findings and responses

## What to Do If You Are Audited

### Step 1: Don't Panic, But Act Quickly
Audit response letters have strict deadlines (typically 30-45 days). Missing deadlines forfeits defense opportunities.

### Step 2: Engage Expert Help
For significant audits, engage experienced healthcare attorneys or billing compliance experts. The cost of expert help is minimal compared to potential recoupments.

### Step 3: Gather Required Documentation
Organize and review requested records before submission. Identify any potential issues proactively.

### Step 4: Submit Complete and Organized Records
Present records professionally. Include:
- Cover letter summarizing the patient care episode
- Organized clinical records in chronological order
- Billing records aligned with clinical records
- Any supporting documentation (labs, imaging, etc.)

### Step 5: Review Audit Findings Carefully
If you disagree with findings, you have appeal rights. Most initial audit findings can be significantly reduced through proper appeals.

## Common Audit Defense Issues

### Inadequate Documentation
**Fix**: CDI programs, documentation training, template optimization

### Medical Necessity Challenges
**Fix**: Physician engagement, clear clinical criteria, thorough notes

### Coding Errors
**Fix**: Certified coders, regular audits, ongoing education

### Authorization Issues
**Fix**: Systematic authorization tracking, pre-service verification

## The Cost of Non-Compliance

Recent audit findings have resulted in:
- Multi-million dollar hospice recoupments for inadequate GIP documentation
- Home health agencies closed after systematic PDGM errors
- Individual providers charged with healthcare fraud
- Exclusion from Medicare and Medicaid participation

The cost of compliance is always lower than the cost of non-compliance.
