The Patient-Driven Groupings Model (PDGM) has fundamentally changed home health reimbursement since 2020. Agencies that master PDGM optimization see 15-25% higher per-period payments than those using outdated billing practices. Here are the 7 strategies that separate top-performing agencies from the rest.

## Strategy 1: Accurate Primary Diagnosis Coding

The primary diagnosis drives the Clinical Grouping, which is the single largest factor in PDGM payment. Use the most specific ICD-10 code that accurately reflects the patient's primary home health need.

**Common coding mistakes:**
- Using symptom codes instead of definitive diagnoses
- Selecting diagnoses based on the hospital discharge summary without verifying clinical relevance
- Missing opportunities for higher-paying clinical groups (like Wound Management or Behavioral Health)

## Strategy 2: Complete Functional Impairment Documentation

The Functional Impairment Level (Low, Medium, High) directly impacts your payment. Many agencies lose revenue because they don't fully document:

- ADL dependencies (bathing, dressing, toileting, transfers, eating)
- Mobility limitations
- Cognitive impairments
- Fall risk factors

## Strategy 3: Capture All Comorbidities

PDGM pays a Comorbidity Adjustment when patients have qualifying secondary diagnoses. Review every patient for:

- Low Comorbidity Adjustment (1 qualifying diagnosis): ~$150 per period
- High Comorbidity Adjustment (2+ interacting diagnoses): ~$300 per period

Missing a comorbidity code can cost $300+ per 30-day period per patient.

## Strategy 4: Timely OASIS Submission

OASIS must be submitted within 30 days of the assessment completion. Late submissions result in automatic payment reductions. Establish a QA workflow that:

- Reviews every OASIS for accuracy within 48 hours
- Flags inconsistencies before transmission
- Tracks submission timelines in real-time

## Strategy 5: Monitor and Prevent LUPAs

A Low Utilization Payment Adjustment (LUPA) pays only per-visit rates instead of the full 30-day period payment. LUPA thresholds vary by clinical group but typically require 3-5 visits per period.

Every LUPA costs your agency $1,500-$3,000 in lost revenue. Strategies to prevent LUPAs:

- Weekly visit utilization monitoring
- Early identification of at-risk patients
- Proactive scheduling adjustments when census drops mid-period

## Strategy 6: Optimize Your Admission Source Coding

Home health payments differ based on whether the admission comes from a community or institutional source. Ensure your intake team correctly documents:

- Community admissions (referrals from physicians, no recent hospitalization)
- Institutional admissions (within 14 days of hospital or SNF discharge)

Institutional admissions generally receive higher payments for the first period.

## Strategy 7: Conduct Monthly PDGM Audits

Review a sample of your billed episodes monthly to catch:

- Coding errors that reduce payment
- Missed comorbidity opportunities
- Documentation gaps
- OASIS inconsistencies with physician orders

## The Bottom Line

PDGM optimization isn't a one-time project — it's an ongoing discipline. Agencies that invest in proper coding, documentation, and monitoring consistently see 20%+ higher per-period reimbursements than their peers.
